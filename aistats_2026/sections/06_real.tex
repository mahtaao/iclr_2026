Two things can be done with the recording itself.

\paragraph{The testable assumption holds.}
The co-modulation fraction of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} is computed on the nine subjects of BCI IV-2a exactly as on the benchmark (Appendix~\ref{app:placement}).
Figure~\ref{fig:real}a places them on axis (d): the subjects sit in the left half of the axis, in a broad band (1--40~Hz) as in the motor band (8--30~Hz), away from the shared-gain regime in which the segment-based methods fail on the benchmark.
The rank condition is therefore not refuted on this data, and a TCL recovery reported on these recordings would pass the only check the recording allows.
This is exactly the situation the paper is about: the check that can be run is passed, and the failures that remain (drift, and nuisance processes with a spatial pattern of their own, Appendix~\ref{app:robust}) are the ones that cannot be seen.

\paragraph{The cue is a reference, and the components do not carry it.}
BCI IV-2a has an experimental manipulation: a randomised four-class motor-imagery cue with a known cortical locus.
We fit each unsupervised method on three runs of a subject without labels and score its components on the other three by how well their per-trial log-variance decodes the cue (Appendix~\ref{app:cue}).
A label-permutation null and a supervised ceiling, common spatial patterns (CSP), bracket the result.
TCL's components decode the cue barely above the permutation null and change substantially between fits on different runs (Figure~\ref{fig:real}b,c).
The linear baselines carry more cue information than TCL, the dependence-based PCL carries the most among the unsupervised methods, and CSP shows that the information is in the recording.
On real EEG the segment-based method found something, and it is not the task-related source.
